## Management interpretation — reviewed for 2Q26

**Itaú — earnings resilience with limited sequential acceleration.** Recurring profit reached BRL 12.4bn, up 7.8% YoY and 1.0% QoQ. Consolidated quarterly ROE was 24.3%; the Brazil-only measure was 25.7% and should not replace it in a consolidated peer table. Management attributes the quarterly client-margin increase to average credit balances, calendar days and liability margins. Credit cost rose 1.9% QoQ while its ratio to average loans stayed at 2.7%. NPL90 remained 1.9% at the release’s rounding precision. [Itau release, PDF p. 5, 8](<../documents/Itau/2026/2T26/Analise_gerencial_da_operacao_e_demonstracoes_contabeis_completas_em_BRGAAP__fcfda4e6561d.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* the central question is how much additional revenue converts to profit as credit and operating costs grow. ROE fell sequentially despite modest profit growth. Track pricing and funding margins alongside average equity, and separate Brazil from LATAM. Avenue consolidation and the 2026 managerial reclassifications are explicit presentation changes, not organic growth by themselves. [Itau release, PDF p. 5](<../documents/Itau/2026/2T26/Analise_gerencial_da_operacao_e_demonstracoes_contabeis_completas_em_BRGAAP__fcfda4e6561d.pdf>)

**Bradesco — continuing recovery, with credit costs still absorbing revenue gains.** Recurring profit reached BRL 7.05bn, up 16.2% YoY and 3.5% QoQ. Management reports ten consecutive quarters of profit growth. Quarterly ROAE was 16.2%; the historical sheet’s 16.0% is the YTD annualized measure. NII grew 15.7% YoY, but expanded provisions grew 22.6%; service fees grew only 1.7%. Insurance operating result increased 8.3% YoY. The bank attributes client-margin growth to credit volumes and spreads, especially liability margins, and explains the NPL increase partly through SME secured working-capital collections. [Bradesco release, PDF p. 1–2](<../documents/Bradesco/2026/2T26/Bradesco_2T26_-_Press_Release__767b88c60a6a.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* recovery is supported by revenue and insurance, but the provision burden warrants attention. Separate the bank and insurer contribution and monitor collateral realization rather than assuming secured lending eliminates loss risk. Accounting profit’s 40.2% QoQ growth is distorted by the BRL 1.781bn non-recurring fiscal charge in 1Q26; recurring growth is 3.5%. [Bradesco release, PDF p. 2](<../documents/Bradesco/2026/2T26/Bradesco_2T26_-_Press_Release__767b88c60a6a.pdf>)

**Banco do Brasil — a quarterly rebound within a weaker earnings profile.** Adjusted profit of BRL 3.91bn rose 13.9% QoQ and 3.3% YoY, but first-half profit fell 34.2%. First-half credit cost grew 43.3%, exceeding NII growth of 12.1%. NPL90 increased from 3.96% in 2Q25 to 5.61%, while coverage fell from 183.2% to 148.5%. The bank reports BRL 421.6bn of agribusiness credit and CET1 of 11.27%. [Banco do Brasil release, PDF p. 1–3](<../documents/Banco_do_Brasil/2026/2T26/Sumario_do_Resultado_2T26__a716c84471fb.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* the modest YoY profit improvement is against an already weak 2Q25 comparison. The risk agenda is credit migration, recoveries and coverage, particularly in agribusiness; quarterly profit recovery alone does not establish that asset quality has turned. The income series shows an adjusted-profit level well below 2024, with an accounting-definition break at 2025 that must remain visible. Attribution of the entire decline to a single sector would require a segment-level loss bridge.

**Santander — adverse client-margin mix and additional provisions.** Recurring profit fell 20.4% QoQ and 17.6% YoY to BRL 3.01bn. Management attributes client-margin pressure to reduced mass-market exposure and lower-spread mix, while market-margin performance improved. Provisions rose 20.6% QoQ, with specific wholesale cases and revised write-off criteria cited. Expenses fell 0.8% QoQ, which was insufficient to offset those pressures. [Santander release, PDF p. 3](<../documents/Santander/2026/2T26/Informe_de_Resultados_2T26__b89f1153b19e.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* revenue mix and credit losses explain more of the immediate challenge than operating-cost growth. A lower NPL90 ratio alongside higher provisions is possible because write-offs, stage migration and specific names affect the measures differently. Ask for the wholesale and write-off bridge before interpreting the small NPL decline as a broad improvement. Goodwill exclusions in ROAE and the accounting-to-managerial reconciliation remain relevant.

**Caixa — margin growth, but a rising credit burden and concentrated mortgage exposure.** Quarterly recurring profit was BRL 3.90bn, up 5.9% YoY; first-half recurring profit fell 17.6%. NII grew 20.2% YoY, while provision expense grew 97.6%. Mortgage loans represented roughly 69.4% of total credit, with 1.45% NPL90; agribusiness was about 4.3% of credit but had a 20.74% NPL90 ratio. Total NPL90 was 3.64%, up 0.98pp YoY. ROE of 9.16% is trailing 12 months. [Caixa performance report, PDF pp. 5–8](<../documents/Caixa/2026/2T26/Relatorio_de_Analise_de_Desempenho_2T26__131132d0814d.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* examine portfolio-weighted losses instead of extrapolating agribusiness delinquency across Caixa’s mortgage-heavy book. NII growth and fees are supporting earnings, but provision growth currently absorbs much of that benefit. CMN 4,966 changes how long troubled loans remain recorded; management explicitly cites this in the NPL comparison. The total credit and customer-deposit series must not be equated with expanded credit and broad funding at other banks.

**BTG Pactual — diversified revenue and scale, with consolidation effects to isolate.** Adjusted profit reached BRL 5.14bn, up 22.6% YoY and 7.0% QoQ; adjusted ROAE was 26.7%. Revenue grew 15.9% YoY and 4.0% QoQ. Corporate Lending and Consumer Finance contributed, while Investment Banking revenue declined 46.1% YoY. Combined AuM/WuM reached BRL 2.7tn, supported by BRL 59bn of quarterly net new money. The release adjusts comparative business-line figures for Consumer Finance & Banking after full ownership of Pan. [BTG Pactual release, PDF p. 3–7](<../documents/BTG_Pactual/2026/2T26/Earnings_Release_2T26__60db3a76d472.pdf>)

*Executive interpretation:* evaluate recurring asset/wealth fees, lending and consumer expansion separately from transactional revenue. Raw balance-sheet growth around Pan consolidation cannot be read as fully organic. The BRL 281.5bn balance-sheet loan measure in the normalized workbook is different from the release’s BRL 366.6bn corporate-plus-consumer portfolio, which uses a broader business definition. Adjusted efficiency is 37.1%, while reported operating expenses include goodwill amortization; dividing those reported expenses by revenue would not reproduce adjusted efficiency. [BTG Pactual release, PDF p. 4–6](<../documents/BTG_Pactual/2026/2T26/Earnings_Release_2T26__60db3a76d472.pdf>)

**CEO/CFO discussion priorities (analytical conclusions):** compare revenue after losses within each bank’s definitions; require explicit loss-migration and write-off bridges; separate insurance and asset-management earnings from credit intermediation; assess capital productivity with a consistent ROE period; and compare funding costs using matched liability perimeters. The evidence supports different management priorities across these business models rather than a single unqualified league table.
